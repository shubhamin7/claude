## What actually matters today

- **Memory thesis split in two (MU, SNDK).** BNP Paribas (Karl Ackerman) downgraded MU to Neutral and cut its target to **$775 from $1,700**. BNP now sees DRAM/NAND supply and demand reaching parity by **mid-2027**, a year earlier than its prior view. The same day, HPE (call on the evening of Nov 23) and Dell (Nov 24) both said component supply is getting *tighter*. HPE expects memory/NAND constraints to last **"at least through the second half of 2028."** Dell said the gap between supply and demand "continues to widen" into next year. MU fell about 10–12% and SNDK about 8–11%. The sell-side bear case is now about 2027–28 parity. Near-term channel data still points to shortage.
- **Pricing is decelerating, though contract prices are still rising.** TrendForce cut its Q4 NAND contract forecast to **+8–13% QoQ (from +15–20%)** and sees **0–5% declines in Q1 2027** (dated Nov 19, context). The NAND wafer spot price has dropped about 24% in six weeks, leaving spot more than 20% below contract. This is the datapoint most relevant to SNDK. Watch it alongside MU's earnings on Dec 17.
- **No greenfield exit trigger.** No commissioning announcement was found. The latest timing on record: SK Hynix Yongin fab 1 targets completion in 1Q27 (production possibly Feb 2027); Micron ID1 first DRAM wafers around mid-2027; Samsung Yongin fab 1 targets Oct 2029. **SK Hynix Yongin is the nearest trigger and should be watched closely.**
- **AI capex demand signal is still strong at the server OEM layer.** Dell booked **$60.2B in AI orders** and has a **record $132.3B AI backlog**. It raised its FY27 AI server revenue forecast to **$78B (from $74B)** and FY27 revenue to **$202B**. Most new orders are for Vera Rubin NVL72. It also added **$25B to its buyback**. Against that, the FT reports that OEMs are repricing signed AI server deals **25–45% higher** because of memory costs (Nov 22, context). That supports memory pricing power, but it is a demand-elasticity risk for end customers.
- **NVDA governance whipsaw.** The board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO (Nov 23), the stock rose about 31%, and a wave of analyst upgrades followed. NVDA then gave back about 6–7% on Nov 24 as congressional scrutiny built: a Senate Banking hearing invitation for **Dec 3** and a House transcribed-interview request. DOJ/SEC investigations continue. AI-hardware names (MRVL, BE, VST) mirrored the round trip.
