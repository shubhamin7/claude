## What actually matters today

- **NVDA beat and raised, and blamed memory costs for lower margins. That supports MU pricing.** Q3 FY27 revenue was $115.8B (+103% y/y) against ~$109B expected. Data center was $107.6B. Q4 guidance is $130B ±3% against ~$125B consensus, with no China DC compute assumed. Gross margin guidance falls to **71–72% from 74.9%**, and Nvidia attributes the drop to higher memory costs. That is a direct read that DRAM/HBM suppliers still hold pricing power at the top of the AI stack. Vera Rubin NVL72 is shipping ("fastest ramp in our history"). Supply commitments are $92B for the rest of FY27, $87B for FY28 and $88B for FY29. Shares were reportedly up about 3.6% after hours (single source).
- **NAND is cracking at the spot level. SNDK is most exposed.** TrendForce (17 Nov) reports NAND spot down ~20% in two weeks. 512Gb wafers fell 32% q/q to $14.30 and 1Tb wafers fell 19% to $24.90. BofA expects **mobile/PC NAND contract prices to fall 5–10% q/q in 1Q27**, with enterprise flat to up. TrendForce still sees enterprise SSD contract prices up 15–20% in 4Q26, but warns that **YMTC's push for share** could cap supplier pricing in 2027. GF Securities also cut its NAND price forecasts. This is a mixed signal against the memory thesis: consumer NAND is weakening while enterprise/AI holds.
- **Greenfield capacity watch: no commissioning event in the window.** No major memory maker announced greenfield fab commissioning (the thesis exit trigger did not fire). On the watch list: **YMTC Fab 3** is slated to start operations later in 2026 and **Fab 4** is targeting ~70% domestic tools. SK hynix's **Yongin Fab 1 phase-1 cleanroom targets 1Q27**. Micron **ID1 first DRAM wafers are ~mid-2027**. None of these is new news.
- **AI capex holds up despite the sell-off.** CNBC tallies: GOOGL kept **$195–205B**, AMZN $220B, META narrowed to $135–145B, and **MSFT cut to $170B from $175B**. Anthropic's S-1/A reportedly lifts its minimum Google compute commitment to **~$121.3B through 2033** (from $114.5B). Anthropic prices its IPO on 18 Nov.
- **Legal and policy overhangs.** The DRAM price-fixing class action against MU, Samsung and SK hynix survived a motion to dismiss (Judge Noel Wise, order dated 14 Nov; answers due 7 Dec). Reuters (17 Nov) reports that the White House is advancing a ban on imports of new Chinese data center equipment, chiefly optical transceivers. That is positive for US optics (MRVL/Lumentum) and a cost headwind for hyperscalers.

**2. DRAM price-fixing class action vs MU, Samsung and SK hynix survives dismissal**
- **Date:** order dated 14 Nov; widely reported 16–17 Nov (SK hynix −1.96% in Seoul on 17 Nov)
- **Source:** [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/sk-hynix-stocks-fall-1-190411984.html) · [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-suit-vs-samsung-sk-hynix-micron-91-5-of-dram-survives/)
- **Summary:**
  - Ruling: Judge Noel Wise (federal court in California, *Garciaguirre v. Samsung Electronics*, 5:26-cv-06345) denied the motion to dismiss. She found that coordinated production cuts and DDR4 phase-outs plausibly plead a Sherman Act conspiracy. She also indicated that the HBM shift alone does not imply collusion.
  - Next steps: answers are due 7 Dec and the first case management conference is 12 Jan 2027.
  - Context: the ruling is procedural, but it is a headline overhang on the pricing narrative.
- **Category:** Legal
