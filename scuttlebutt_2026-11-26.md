## What actually matters today

- **The memory thesis is under real stress.** TrendForce (Nov 19) now forecasts NAND contract prices **down 0–5% QoQ in 1Q27**, with all categories entering a downcycle from 2Q27. It also cut its 4Q26 forecast to +8–13% (from +15–20% in September). The drivers are Chinese CSP eSSD order cuts, 25–30% bit-supply growth in 2027, and NAND capex +26.7% to $29.9B. NAND wafer spot prices are down more than 20%. **The result:** SNDK is roughly 75% below its June peak (closed $73.61 on Nov 25, post 8:1 split), and MU closed $601.25 on Nov 25, down about 33.5% from Oct 26. DRAM contract prices are still rising (TrendForce 4Q26 +10–15%, secondary source).
- **Greenfield capacity is getting closer. Watch closely; the exit trigger is not yet tripped.**
  - JKN reported on Nov 25, citing Hanwha, that **SK hynix has begun placing equipment orders for Yongin Phase 1**. The first cleanroom opens in early 2027, first wafer output is expected in 4Q27, and Hanwha sees end-2027 capacity of about 100k wafers/month. SK hynix has not confirmed the orders.
  - Hanwha expects Samsung P5 to follow.
  - This is not yet commissioning, but the trigger date now has a visible path to 4Q27.
- **Analysts are cutting memory targets.**
  - MU: BNP Paribas Exane **downgraded to Neutral** with a $775 target (from $1,700); its thesis is supply/demand balance in mid-2027 rather than mid-2028. UBS cut to $1,100 (from $1,625) and kept Buy.
  - SNDK: Evercore cut to $175 (from $200) and kept Outperform. Insider selling (C-suite Form 4s and a Form 144) added to the pressure.
- **Nvidia: export restrictions and governance in the same 24 hours.**
  - The FT reported (Nov 26) that **Nvidia told Dell, Supermicro and other partners to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia**, after a new Commerce rule requiring licenses (cloud providers get 90 days to apply).
  - S&P put NVDA's AA rating on **CreditWatch Negative** (Nov 25), citing governance concerns after Jensen Huang's ouster and Nov 23 reinstatement.
  - This hits DELL directly as a shipping partner.
- **AI capex demand signals are mixed.**
  - DELL's AI backlog hit a **record $132.3B** (Nov 24 print, edge of window).
  - Against that, Kuo's report (Nov 20) says Google cut 2027 TPU 8i units by about 1M, shifting volume to MediaTek's 8t because of supply and power constraints.
  - GOOGL touched a 52-week low on Nov 25 on AI-debt worries, and BE fell about 10% in the same AI-infrastructure selloff.

## Nvidia (NVDA)

1. **FT: Nvidia halts server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia** — Nov 26 [in window]. *[Regulatory/geopolitical; Demand]*
   - Partners including Dell and Supermicro were told to hold orders, according to three people cited by the FT.
   - The trigger is a new Commerce rule requiring licenses for advanced AI chips and servers sent to those four countries plus Brazil. Cloud providers get 90 days to apply and prove their customers are not restricted entities.

   - Senators are also pushing to freeze Nvidia export licenses after DOJ smuggling charges.
