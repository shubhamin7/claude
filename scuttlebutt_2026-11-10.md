## What actually matters today

- **Memory-cycle thesis — sell-side is now openly modelling the exit.** Morgan Stanley (Shawn Kim) cut SK hynix's target 38% and Samsung's 19%. The note argues that new memory capacity starting **from late 2027** will meet a slowing data-center build. MS cut its forecast for 2028 global new DC capacity growth to **9% from 21%** and assumes ~5 GW of 2028 projects slip to 2029. It also cut SK hynix's 2029–35 operating margin forecast to 28% from 53%. This is the "greenfield capacity meets demand air-pocket" scenario our MU/SNDK exit trigger is built around. No fab has been commissioned yet (SK hynix Yongin is still on track for its first cleanroom in Feb 2027 and output in Oct 2027), but the timing debate has moved into consensus.
- **NAND is cracking before DRAM.** The latest TrendForce data shows 512Gb TLC wafer spot **−4.33% w/w** (week of 2 Nov) and DDR4 spot flat to softer, while Q4 contract guidance still says up (DRAM +10–15%, NAND +15–20%). SNDK fell another 3.6% Tuesday as coverage pointed to softer NAND pricing and more cautious OEM buying. Counterpoint has **YMTC (14%) now ahead of SanDisk (11%)** in NAND revenue share. Watch whether spot weakness reaches Q1'27 contract talks.
- **NVDA governance and China-export shock is spreading to the whole AI chain.** The Information reports that the alleged Chinese intelligence asset linked to Jensen Huang may have helped steer **≥$7.5B** of Nvidia chip shipments through Chinese companies. NVDA fell another ~5.5% (briefly under $4T market cap), following the board's investigation, an FBI inquiry, a Rosen class action and a House briefing request for 18 Nov. DigiTimes flags lawmakers pushing tighter China AI-chip export curbs, which adds **HBM demand uncertainty** for Samsung and SK hynix (and so for MU). NVDA reports Q3 on 17 Nov.
- **Vendor financing of the AI customer: the Anthropic IPO private placement.** Anthropic set its IPO range at **$70–78** (825M shares, Nasdaq "ANT"), pricing 18 Nov and trading 19 Nov per CNBC sources. It also disclosed a **$26B Class N placement** from its suppliers at the IPO price: **NVDA $10B, GOOGL $10B, MU $1B**, plus AMD $2.5B, AVGO $1B, MSFT $1B and TSMC (~$0.5B). The revised S-1 reportedly shows **$895B of purchase commitments**. This supports AI demand, but it also means suppliers are funding their own demand.
- **GOOGL is taking share from NVDA.** On Monday Google added the TPU 8tx (confidential inference, AMD EPYC hosts) and the TPU 8i Max (two chips per package, double the HBM bandwidth). Google says it has nearly 100 TPU customers and kept its 2026 capex guide at $195–205B. This is incrementally more HBM per package, which helps MU even as NVDA's share comes under question.
