## What actually matters today

- **Memory pricing is split, and this is the thesis-critical signal.** TrendForce (Nov 17, **[in window]**) reports NAND spot prices down ~20% in two weeks on weak consumer demand and YMTC share-taking. BofA data cited in the same piece: 512Gb wafers −32% QoQ to $14.30, 1Tb −19% to $24.90. BofA also sees smartphone/PC NAND contract prices falling 5–10% QoQ in 1Q27, with enterprise NAND flat to up. TrendForce still has enterprise SSD contract prices +15–20% QoQ in 4Q26. **Direct watch item for SNDK; secondary for MU.** Consumer NAND is rolling over while AI/enterprise holds.
- **Nvidia beat and raised: AI capex demand is intact.** FQ3 revenue was $115.8B and data center revenue $107.6B (+110% YoY). FQ4 guide is $130B ±3% vs. ~$125B consensus, with zero China data center compute assumed. The FY28 growth outlook was raised to 75% from 70%. Supply commitments increased mainly on **multi-year memory procurement** (reported as $279B → ~$390B; verify in the 10-Q). This supports MU/SNDK and the HBM/DRAM side even as consumer NAND spot weakens. Vera Rubin NVL72 is shipping to Google Cloud, Azure, OCI, CoreWeave and Nebius.
- **YMTC supply signal (indirect NAND risk).** YMTC's third Wuhan fab is due to start operations later in 2026, producing >300-layer NAND. It targets ~70% domestic tooling for fab 4 and a Shanghai IPO as early as 1Q27. TrendForce says its bit-share push may cap NAND price increases in early 2027. **This is not a major-memory-maker greenfield commissioning, so the defined exit trigger is NOT tripped.** It is worth tracking as the nearest thing to it.
- **Capacity timing: no new commissioning found.** The latest available guidance is unchanged: SK hynix Yongin phase 1 in 1Q27 (Feb 2027 per TrendForce, July report) and Samsung's first Yongin fab in 2029. Nothing new surfaced on Samsung P5, Kioxia, or Micron Idaho/NY.
- **Nvidia governance and export overhang continue.** Jensen Huang resigned Nov 12 and Colette Kress is interim CEO; the board will not reinstate him. Commerce's Nov 16 interim final rule closes the remote-access (cloud-rental) loophole for Chinese firms and adds license requirements for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil. Post-print analyst actions were mixed: Wedbush and JPMorgan raised targets, Jefferies cut.
