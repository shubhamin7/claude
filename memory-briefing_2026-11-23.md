## Key supply/demand developments
- **TrendForce cut its NAND contract outlook (11-19).** 4Q26 is now +8–13% (was +15–20%), 1Q27 is 0 to −5%, and all categories are forecast to fall from 2Q27. 2027 bit supply is forecast at +25–30% YoY and capex +26.7% to $29.9B. Chinese CSPs cut 2027 enterprise SSD orders by more than US CSPs raised theirs.
- **The NAND spot collapse has largely played out.** 512Gb TLC wafer is down about 23% in four weeks to $14.24, but the latest week was only −0.20%. TrendForce says the spot-contract gap is now more than 20%.
- **DRAM is still tight.** DDR5 16Gb spot ticked up to $60.67, while DDR4 spot keeps easing (−1.8% WoW). Micron's CEO said on 11-18 that 2027–28 supply will be "even tighter" than 2026 and that Micron is already negotiating 2028 volumes ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/19/microns-ceo-says-2027-and-2028-will-be-even-tighter-than-this-year/)). KB Securities forecasts 2027 contract prices up 40% for DRAM and 30% for NAND.
- **Brokers split on NAND.** GF Securities cut its NAND price forecast and warned of a 2027 glut; SNDK fell 4% on 11-17 ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/17/sandisk-and-micron-fall-4-as-gf-securities-cuts-nand-price-forecast-and-warns-of-a-glut-sk-hynix-dips/)). BofA expects PC and smartphone NAND contract prices to fall 5–10% QoQ in 1Q27.
- **No new HBM wafer-allocation data this week.** The working baseline is still HBM at about 22% of DRAM wafer input in 2026 and about 30% in 2027 (TrendForce via [iconnect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/150239/tight-dram-supply-boosts-pricing-power-hbm-contract-prices-set-to-surge-in-2027/150236/design)).

## Thesis read
This week **splits the thesis: DRAM support holds, NAND support weakens**. On DRAM, the HBM wafer-diversion leg still holds. No greenfield DRAM capacity reached commissioning, Micron is talking about 2027–28 being tighter, and DDR5 spot is firm. On NAND, TrendForce's 11-19 call undercuts the thesis without any greenfield fab. It forecasts 1Q27 contract declines and a 2Q27 downturn across all categories, driven by **+25–30% bit growth from existing fabs** (layer migration and QLC) plus **demand cuts** from Chinese CSPs and consumer devices. "No greenfield NAND capacity" is therefore still true, but it no longer guarantees NAND tightness. Treat the NAND leg as impaired. Keep the exit trigger at NO, and consider adding a second NAND tripwire: TrendForce or CFM confirming an actual 1Q27 contract decline.
