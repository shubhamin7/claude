## What actually matters today
- **AI capex fear eased, and the trigger was CoreWeave, not a hyperscaler.** CoreWeave's Q3 8-K (Nov 10, after the close):
  - FY26 capex is now $37–39B (prior range $35–39B), because it expects to deliver infrastructure to customers sooner than planned.
  - FY26 revenue guidance is now $13.0–13.3B, and the exit-2026 run-rate is $20–21B.

  This drove a broad rebound in the watchlist names on Nov 11: MU +~13%, SNDK +~17%, DELL +~14%, BE +~17%. It followed a roughly 40–50% drawdown in several of these names after the Nov 3 midterms raised fears of data-center regulation. **This is a sentiment reversal, not new end-demand data from MSFT, GOOGL or META.**
- **Memory pricing (thesis-critical) is mixed at the edge.** TrendForce (Nov 11):
  - PC DRAM contract prices rise **13–18% QoQ in 4Q26**, a slowdown from 18–23% in 3Q26.
  - OEM DRAM inventory rose to **7–10 weeks** (from 6–9) and is expected to keep growing. Notebook shipments fell about 14–16% QoQ in 3Q26.
  - Offsets: Korean suppliers plan to cut **PC DRAM output by more than 20% YoY in 2027** to prioritize server DRAM. The overall 4Q26 forecast is unchanged: conventional DRAM +10–15%, NAND +15–20%.

  Read: the consumer and PC channel is softening while server and AI demand holds. Watch this for the first crack in the pricing thesis.
- **Capacity timing: no exit trigger tripped.** No greenfield fab commissioning by a major memory maker was found in the window. Two background items:
  - Micron's paused Taoyuan Fab 11E (Nov 5, labor dispute) *removes* future supply rather than adding it.
  - A report (date unverified) says YMTC pulled its third Wuhan fab forward to 2H26 from 2027. It is an indirect NAND-supply signal and worth verifying.
- **NVDA governance overhang is the largest company-specific risk on the list.**
  - Baird downgraded NVDA to Neutral and cut its target to **$200 from $500**, citing CEO uncertainty plus 2027 slowdown and supply worries.
  - Morgan Stanley dropped NVDA from its Top Pick list but kept Overweight and a $300 target.
  - The board retained Wachtell and Joon Kim (ex-acting SDNY US Attorney) to run its probe.
  - NYT-based reports say Commerce paused its Megaspeed chip-diversion probe, and DOJ took it over, after Nvidia's general counsel contacted officials.
- **Anthropic IPO private placement ($26B) ties three watchlist names to one customer.** Per a Nov 10 filing: NVDA $10B, GOOGL $10B, MU $1B, alongside AMD, AVGO, MSFT and TSMC. Pricing is expected around Nov 18. It reads as a demand-visibility positive, but the "circular financing" critique is getting airtime.
