## What actually matters today
- **Export controls tightened (AI capex / regulatory, cross-cutting).** On Nov 16, BIS issued an interim final rule that closes the "remote access" loophole. It adds license requirements for advanced AI chips and servers shipped to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, and for any data center worldwide that may provide compute to China or other restricted parties. It comes ahead of a Trump–Xi summit in Shenzhen. NVDA, MU, SNDK, MRVL, BE and DELL all sold off. Benzinga put tech market-cap losses at about $960B. **The rule is chip- and server-focused; I found no HBM-specific provision**, so for MU and SNDK the hit runs through second-order demand.
- **DRAM antitrust case survives dismissal (MU, legal).** On Nov 14, Judge Noel Wise (N.D. Cal.) denied the joint Micron/Samsung/SK hynix motion to dismiss the consumer DRAM class action. The judge found the alleged synchronized January 2026 "customer-vetting" practice supports a plausible inference of agreement, and rejected other theories. Defendants must respond by Dec 7, and the first case management conference is Jan 12, 2027. MU fell about 6–7% on Nov 16. Risk is mainly headline and multiple pressure for now; the ruling makes no liability finding.
- **Memory capacity timing — no exit trigger tripped, but watch SK hynix Y1.** No greenfield fab was commissioned in the window. Reporting from Nov 11–16 still has SK hynix Yongin Y1 targeting operation around **Feb 2027**; the in-fab "Trinity" supplier test fab is near complete. Samsung P5 Phase 1 equipment move-in is being reviewed for **Q2 2027**, ahead of the original schedule. Micron Idaho Fab 1 is still mid-2027. **Y1 is the nearest potential trigger and is roughly 3 months out.**
- **Memory pricing is still rising but decelerating.** TrendForce's Nov 11 update has 4Q26 PC DRAM contract prices rising 13–18% QoQ, down from 18–23% in 3Q. Q4 conventional DRAM is +10–15%, NAND is +15–20%, and spot DDR4 is stalling. Combined with Kioxia's flat December-quarter guidance (Oct 30), this is the "rate of change is peaking" debate behind SNDK's 41% one-month drawdown.
- **NVDA reports Tue Nov 17. It is the first print since Jensen Huang resigned** (Nov 11–12; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO). Consensus is about $109B revenue and $2.47 EPS, with options implying about ±10.6%. The read-through matters for MU/SNDK (HBM/SSD), DELL (reports Nov 24) and MRVL (reports late Nov or Dec 1; dates conflict).
